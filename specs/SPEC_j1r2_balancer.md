## 2. Mechanism and why S2 must NOT be yoke-anchored
Zero-length-type spring (F = k (|AB| - L0), cord over a swivel sheave, 2:1 tackle, compression spring in a guide tube) between **A fixed on the pad** and **B on the upper arm**. Moment on the arm = (B - P1) x F, F = k (A - B). With A above P1 this equals the gravity moment of the arm about the S1' axis (y-lever) AND about the S2 axis (proportional to sin phi cos th1), so the cos th1 coupling is automatic. A baseline-style yoke-anchored S2 spring gives only K sin phi, independent of th1: with a separate phase-shifted S1' spring (K 3.9 N.m, phase -55 deg) the best decoupled pair leaves **S1' 4.34 N.m (SF 1.91, FAILS 2.0)**, S2 3.55 (SF 2.34). The real mechanism for the yoke case would be a gimbal that counter-rotates the anchor by -th1 (extra parts); the pad anchor avoids it.
Parameters: A = P1 + (-10.0, -19.7, +73.4) mm (world (-55.0, -89.7, +38.4); r 76 mm from the S1' axis, 105 deg in the yz plane, beside the S1' motor R44.5 so the cap (R28) clears it by 3.5+ mm); B = arm local (-15.6, -42.3, 118.4) (lug 13 mm off the R32 tube); k_cord 0.752 N/mm; L0 54.6 mm; cord length 60..200 mm; **F_cord max 109 N (SF 10.6 vs 1156 N usable Dyneema 1.5 mm), spring force max 218 N at 2:1**. Search: DE over A, B, k, L0 on the full grid with penalties (anchor on the pad rim 70..110 mm, cord <= 200 mm, force <= 260 N, anchor sector 105..230 deg so it is outside the S2 motor's swept sector); the unconstrained optimum (A 112 mm above, 2.59 N.m) was rejected: it needs a 112 mm pylon.
